# VFX & animation rework

Reference notes for the presentation pass. Gameplay numbers, progression, damage and the
network protocol are unchanged apart from one widened wind-up condition (noted below).

## Locked content

WiFi Haki and the Conqueror's Release are **untouched**, verified three ways:

- none of `wifiHakiArc`, `conquerorCharge`, `conquerorRelease`, `addConquerorRangeLightning`,
  `fullChargeConquerorBurst`, `fullChargeConquerorDome`, `supremePressureFronts`, `skyBreak`,
  `skyRift` or `horizonCracks` appears anywhere in the diff;
- all eight legacy sprites (`soft`, `spark`, `beam`, `coating`, `conqueror_soft`, `rage_flame`,
  `rage_glow`, `rage_lightning_beam`) are byte-identical, so anything still drawing from them
  renders exactly as before;
- the shared camera-shake path was changed only inside the `lockView` branch, which is set solely
  for `kings_grip_*` sequences. The `else` branch used by `supreme_conqueror`, `galaxy_charge` and
  `galaxy_impact` is mathematically identical to the original.

`wifi_haki.json`, `conqueror_release*.json` and `supreme_conqueror_release.json` are unmodified.

## Why the particles looked bad

Three separate causes, all addressed:

| Cause | Was | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sprites | `soft.png` a 64px linear ramp peaking at alpha 243 — large clouds showed a hard circular rim; `beam.png` a 32x8 image filled with a uniform alpha of 211, so **every bolt in the mod was a hard-edged rectangle** | 13 procedural sprites with gaussian falloff, fully dead rims, fbm break-up, tapered jagged bolts |
| Behaviour | constant size and constant colour — particles appeared at full size and vanished at their lifetime cutoff | keyframed size/colour envelopes; particles are born, breathe and die |
| Blending | alpha only — overlapping energy went grey | additive for anything emitting light |

Sprites are generated by `tools/generate_effect_textures.py` (pure stdlib, deterministic).
`tools/preview_effect_textures.py` renders the contact sheet.

`HakiCurves` implements Photon's `NumberFunction` directly rather than hand-packing cubic-bezier
control points into `Curve`/`Gradient`, so envelopes are readable keyframes. Both over-lifetime
channels **multiply** in Photon: size 1 -> 0 shrinks, and an ARGB ramp `0xFFFFFFFF -> 0x00FFFFFF`
fades alpha while preserving tint.

## Galaxy Impact

Previously the charge was a glow on the fist; the source explicitly noted "never an overhead
galaxy". It now manifests one, which is the technique's actual read in the source material.

`GalaxyRenderer` draws it as world-space geometry, not particles — a spiral is a *structure*, and
billboarded sprites cannot produce arms, a bulge and dust lanes. Three counter-rotating discs give
depth, a camera-facing core gives it a nucleus, and spiral inflow ribbons connect the rim to the
charging fist. Each disc is billboarded then squashed on its vertical axis, so every viewer sees
the spiral face while the foreshortening keeps it reading as an inclined disc; the sprite rotates
inside that fixed ellipse, which is how a spinning inclined disc actually behaves.

Timing is derived locally from the server's fixed `GALAXY_READY_TICK = 88` timeline and driven off
the existing `GALAXY_FIST_START/INTENSIFY/STOP` packets, so there is **no protocol change**.
`FIST_STOP` fires on the punch frame, seven ticks before contact, which is exactly where the
galaxy should be dragged down into the fist.

The detonation's seven shock rings were 190-360 sprites each scattered around a circle; each is
now a single expanding ring billboard. That reads as a continuous wave rather than a dotted one
and removes roughly two thousand particles per detonation.

## King's Grip

The camera was one locked-off profile plate with shake and orbiting deliberately disabled. A
camera that never reacts tells the viewer nothing is happening, which is what made the execution
read as staged. Recut as a shot list: arrival, a slow dolly-in through the neck lift with a
creeping dutch angle, a snap to a low wide-lens chamber at the wind-up, a compression push, a
two-keyframe freeze on contact (the hitstop), then a violent blow-out and wide settle. Beats are
driven from the server's own tier timings.

Shake is now three incommensurate frequencies per axis instead of one sine, which is most of the
difference between a mechanical wobble and a camera with an operator.

The victim was the other half of the problem: on the Advanced tier the hold went from
`body.pitch = -8` at tick 24 to `-10` at tick 112 — two degrees across 4.4 seconds of screen time.
`tools/generate_kings_grip_struggle.py` fills the hold with a struggle that fights then fades
(leg kicks, both arms hauling at the hand on the throat, head thrashing, decaying into limpness),
leaving the authored catch and recoil keyframes alone since those are timed against the server's
impact tick.

**One behaviour change:** the wind-up pulse now fires on every tier rather than Advanced alone, so
all three versions show air converging into the cocked fist and ground drag. Advanced still
exclusively owns the bone-attached left-fist fire.

## Blade cuts

Were untextured `POSITION_COLOR` quads, so every cut showed its polygon silhouette, and drew with
depth testing off so they punched through terrain. The band now spans the arc and extends
*backwards* from a leading rim — the old band's thickness ran perpendicular to travel, which gave
a texture nothing meaningful to describe — and samples `crescent.png` with `v = 0` pinned to the
honed edge. Adds decaying afterimages along the travelled path and layered shell/core/edge passes.
Depth testing is on.

## Advanced Haki

`PehkuiBridge` thickens the model while Advanced Haki is active, scaled by Armament mastery. It
drives **model** scale types only, so hitbox, reach and eye height are untouched and it cannot
affect balance or hit registration. Bound entirely through reflection and gated on `ModList`, so
Pehkui stays a soft dependency and its absence is a silent no-op.

# Patch 2

## King's Grip camera (bug)

The shot drifted out of frame and the two participants never saw the same thing. Three causes,
all in the same line: the offset went through `Camera.move()` at the tail of `setup()`.

1. `move()` runs *after* vanilla has already pulled the third-person camera back by
   `getMaxZoom(4.0)`, which **clips against terrain**. The identical shot started up to four
   blocks further out in the open than it did against a wall. That is the drift.
2. `move()` works in the camera's own basis, so the offset rotated with the view rather than
   staying locked to the two bodies.
3. Its origin is the local camera entity — the attacker on one client, the suspended victim on
   the other.

The shot is now built from the grip pair and placed absolutely. Keyframe offsets read as
(dolly along the pair axis, height, distance out to the side); the attacker/victim sign flip
cancels the victim's 180° yaw, and the victim's mid-height is lowered by exactly the lift the
server applies (`pinGrabPair`, 0.38 for a 1.8-high player). Both clients therefore resolve to the
**same world position and world yaw to twelve decimal places** — verified against every keyframe.
Pitches are derived from each keyframe's own height and distance so the pair stays centred, and
the placement is terrain-clipped so an absolute position cannot bury the view in a wall.

Every other sequence still uses the original relative `move()` path.

## Conqueror's Haki

A committed full-charge release now roars: coil, then head thrown back with the chest opened and
arms flung down and out, held on a decaying tremble. Joy Boy keeps its authored supreme pose.
Victims read as being in pain rather than merely having fallen over — longer stuns get a
hands-to-the-head double-over or a back-arched convulsion that weakens as they go limp, while
short taps keep the sharp knockdowns so a tap still feels like a hit rather than a scene.
**SFX untouched**, as asked.

## Physique

`muscle_overlay.png` is authored in the standard skin UV layout and contains only shading —
pectoral mass, sternum split, the shadow under the chest, an ab ladder, lat flare, deltoid caps.
Re-rendering the real body and arm ModelParts with it paints definition over any skin, and because
it rides the actual parts it deforms with Player Animator and BendyLib. It is encoded pure black
and white with all strength in alpha so it can only darken or lighten; a mid-grey encoding would
wash pale and dark skins toward grey. Chest is weighted highest. Definition and Pehkui bulk both
now scale across every stage — coating, Ryuo, Advanced — rather than Advanced alone.

## Tooling

`tools/check_self_calls.py` guards against the failure mode that broke build #5: a whole-block
rewrite silently deleting a helper other code still calls. It compares declared methods against
unqualified call sites per class, ignoring comments and string literals. Run against the rejected
commit it reproduces all three of CI's errors and nothing else.

Note for future edits: `validateHakiResources` scans **line-bounded**, so a multi-line
`animate(...)` call silently drops every clip named inside it from the build's checks. Pose
selection is kept as single-line `String …Pose = …;` assignments for that reason.

# Patch 3

## Boot crash (fixed)

Three generated clips wrote a `bend` key on `head`. Player Animator resolves each channel to a
`StateCollection` field and calls `addKeyFrame` on it with no null check, so naming a channel a
part does not have throws an NPE **during resource load** — crashing the client before the main
menu rather than at the point of use. Only torso, arms and legs bend.

`tools/check_animations.py` now validates every clip's channels against the part schema, plus tick
ordering and the `bend` requirement.

## King's Grip camera (fixed properly)

Patch 2 moved the shot to absolute placement but used `right` where vanilla's `Camera.move()`
takes its horizontal argument along `left`. The camera sat beside the pair **facing directly
away** — hence "can't even see what's happening". Verified numerically that every keyframe now
aims at the pair and that both participants still resolve to the same world position.

Recut to the brief: close on the throat grab → cut to the cocked fist → blow out wide to the full
picture, with the hitstop preserved.

# Patch 5

## Conqueror's charge (remade)

The charge rendered as a contortion. It swung the legs off the ground (pitch -46 / +23) while also
yawing and rolling them 12-15 degrees *and* bending the knees 46, so the lower body folded in three
directions at once and the feet ended up pointing sideways. Player Animator will happily draw a
pose no skeleton could hold.

Rebuilt around the rule that a body cannot brace and kick at the same time: the legs stay planted
and pointing forward (yaw 0 throughout) and their motion comes from the knees, which load and pump
with the laugh while the weight rocks between the feet. All the openness is in the upper body --
chest thrown open, head back, both arms flung out behind. The release loads one beat further back
and then drives forward off a planted lead leg, so the roar comes from the ground up.

## King's Grip (remade)

The originals froze. From tick 20 to tick 66 the body, torso, legs and holding arm held *identical*
values — two and a half seconds of a cinematic playing as a still image — and the punch was a
single two-tick jump from that still into full extension. Nothing anticipated it and nothing
followed it, which is exactly why it read as weightless.

All six clips (three tiers, both sides) are now generated rather than hand-authored, and the
attacker's is authored as a moving pose throughout:

| Beat | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Grip | The arm shoots out, the hand closes, and the victim's weight arrives a beat *late* — the shoulder gives before it sets |
| Hold | Breathes and strains: the arm trembles, the shoulder sags and re-catches, and the tremor grows the longer the victim hangs there. The free hand is already drifting back |
| Chamber | Coils on an accelerating curve (`t²`), hips turning away so the punch has somewhere to come from, head staying locked on the victim while the body turns off them |
| Punch | A kinetic chain over 7 ticks: hips fire first (body yaw +14 while the torso still trails at -4), torso catches them, then the shoulder, then the fist. That lag *is* the heft |
| Contact | Compresses rather than freezing — the old hitstop repeated one frame byte-for-byte, which reads as a paused video; this reads as strain |
| Follow-through | The arm overshoots, the torso keeps rotating and the back foot swings through before it settles |

Keyframe count per attacker clip went from 13 to 24-36 depending on tier.

The victim gained a struggle of its own: legs kicking on a separate beat from the hands clawing at
the wrist, with the fight visibly draining out of them before the fist lands.

### Fist particles

The chamber pulses every 8 ticks with a rising ramp, but the effect ignored that ramp entirely, so
every pulse looked identical and the fist never read as loading toward anything. Density now rides
it, and three layers were added where the eye is actually looking:

- a tight orbiting swarm on the knuckles (the old chamber gathered air from a metre out and left
  the hand itself bare);
- a shell that collapses onto the fist, so each pulse visibly tightens;
- short arcs wrapping the knuckles, at every tier — the ground bolts stay Haki-gated.

On contact the fist gets its own detonation at the knuckles: flash, radial sparks, a displaced-air
cone, a tight shockring and arcs. Deliberately small and dense — the wide part of the impact is
already the directional air sheet and the ground reaction, and a third large shell just washed the
frame out.

## Physique (third attempt, and the reason for the first two)

Scaling the `body` ModelPart was wrong for a reason worth writing down: vanilla's torso is a single
8x12x4 box covering chest **and** waist, so widening the part widens the belly in step with the
chest. That is why it read as fat rather than built.

A built torso is a taper, so it needs a seam vanilla does not have. `HakiChestLayer` supplies one:
a second, larger copy of only the **top six rows** of the torso box, drawn over the real one. Being
strictly larger it encloses and hides the original chest, while the bottom six rows keep their own
width — the step where the shell ends is the taper. Its cube sits at the body's own
`texOffs(16, 16)` with height 6, so its faces land on exactly the chest half of the skin's torso
UVs: it is the player's own skin, not a tint. A matching jacket cube keeps the second skin layer.

Arms scale by ModelPart as before, at 22% against the chest's 28% — arms that outgrow their own
torso read as a costume. Depth grows at 70% of width, so the shape is a broad chest and not a
barrel. Head, waist and legs are untouched.

# Patch 7

The chest was widening into the arms rather than past them. Vanilla's arms sit flush against a
4-texel torso half-width, so anything the shell gained beyond that was simply hidden behind the
shoulders — the build was there, it just could not be seen from the front.

Each arm now moves outward by three terms added together, since three things push it into the
chest at once: how far the shell grew past the arm's inner face, how far the arm's own widening
grew *inward* from its pivot, and a deliberate clearance so the two are not left flush. The real
pivots are captured in `Pre` and restored verbatim in `Post` rather than subtracting an offset —
`PlayerModel#setupAnim` runs between the two, so restoring by subtraction would corrupt the shared
part if anything downstream rewrote `x`. Sleeves need no handling: `setupAnim` copies them from the
arms.

Gains raised for a visible difference: chest 28% → **55%** width (depth at 65% of that), arms
22% → **40%**. At full Advanced Haki and 1000 mastery each arm sits about three texels clear of the
body, so the widened chest and the separation between it and the deltoid both read at a glance.

# Patch 8

## Conqueror's hold: no legs at all

Patch 5 planted the legs and drove them from the knees. That was still wrong, and the reason is
worth writing down so it is not attempted a third time: **any** scripted leg pose fights what the
player is actually doing. They may be walking, standing on a slab, mid-fall or on a staircase, and
Player Animator will draw the authored pose over all of it, so the lower body ends up folded in
directions no skeleton holds.

The Conqueror clips — `conqueror_gear5_charge`, `conqueror_gear5_release` and `conqueror_scream` —
now emit **no leg channels whatsoever**. Omitting a channel leaves that limb under vanilla control,
which is always correct, and is the same thing `conqueror_tap_point` already did.

The charge is now what it should have been: both forearms folded up over the face, head thrown
back, and the whole chest convulsing on a fast cackle with a slower sway under it. The release
tears the hands off the face and flings them wide as the chest drives forward through the shout.

## Build fix

`RenderPlayerEvent#getRenderer()` already returns `PlayerRenderer`, so `instanceof PlayerRenderer`
against it is a compile error ("expression type is a subtype of pattern type"), not a widening
cast. The previous code only compiled because it passed through a parameter typed
`EntityRenderer<? extends AbstractClientPlayer>`. Both sites take the model directly now.

# Patch 9

## Counter punch: a lunge, not a jog

Reference: a long low lunge — front knee driven up and folded under the chest, trailing leg
stretched straight out behind, chest dropped toward the target, punching arm at full length and the
free arm counterweighting it straight back.

The clip charged in upright with a fist held out in front, which reads as running into someone
rather than striking them. Ticks 27-32 are that lunge now: `rightLeg` at +68 with bend 78 folding
under the body, `leftLeg` at -54 with bend 4 stretched out behind, body pitched 26 degrees forward
and dropped, `rightArm` at -108 with no elbow bend, `leftArm` swung back to +62. Contact compresses
over the lead knee and kicks the trailing foot up behind from the stop.

## Arrow throw: whole body

The grab was fine; the throw was an arm action, so the arrow looked handed over rather than thrown.
It is a whole-body throw now, with the same kinetic chain as any real one:

- the hips wind away first, weight onto the back leg, front foot off the ground, and the arm ends up
  cocked past the ear *because of the turn* rather than on its own;
- the hips and front foot fire while the arm is still behind them — that lag is the power;
- release with everything aligned behind the hand and the front leg planted hard;
- and a follow-through that carries the hand down and across the body. Stopping the hand at the
  release was most of why it looked weak.

`ARROW_CATCH_HOLD_TICKS` is cut to the clip's release frame (34), so the projectile leaves on the
exact tick the hand does — it was launching two ticks early, before the arm had come through.
Return speed raised to 3.1 → 5.0 across Observation mastery.

# Patch 10

## The held arrow

Two things made the caught shot unreadable in the hand.

`setCritArrow(true)` was applied at catch time, and `AbstractArrow` spawns a CRIT particle every
tick it is marked crit — so the shaft was buried in a cloud of them, which was all you could
actually see. Crit now goes on at the throw, where it belongs.

The arrow also kept whatever direction it had been flying, and a shot frozen along its incoming
path is usually aimed at or away from whoever is watching, so it foreshortens into a few pixels.
It stands vertical now, the one orientation that shows its full length from every angle.

That orientation is produced by giving it a hair of upward velocity rather than by writing `xRot`.
`AbstractArrow#tick` recomputes its own rotation from its motion every tick and lerps toward the
result, so a written angle is dragged back out within a few ticks — the arrow's own maths has to
arrive at vertical for it to stay there. The 0.0016 of drift that produces is re-pinned away on the
same tick.

## The flip

Faster, and the rotation now visibly completes. The full 360 is spent in the first thirteen ticks
at a steady ~28 degrees a tick, and the remaining airtime is upright with the legs reaching for the
ground, rather than the rotation easing out somewhere near the top. The launch is tightened from
0.82 to 0.66 (peak ~2.8 blocks, ~16 ticks of air) so the whole counter reads quicker while staying
well clear of the swing; the landing, cock, charge and strike ticks all move up with it — 16, 19,
22 and 28.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Draw | 0 → 36 / 44 / 52 | Fist chambers low and behind while the free hand hauls. A vortex converges on the fist and drags the target in on an accelerating curve |

## Why it is scripted rather than physical

The attacker has to be reliably *above* a target that is already in the air. A launch left to
velocity lands somewhere different on every surface, ping and hitbox, so both bodies are driven to
scripted positions each tick: the target on a decelerating climb and an accelerating drop (the
mirror of each other), the attacker planted, then holding above the column, then riding it down a
beat behind. `tools/generate_haki_animations.py` and `HakiServerController.thraggBeats` carry the
same numbers, and the clips are cut to the server's schedule rather than the other way round.

## Damage

The old single hit is split 40 / 35 / 25 across uppercut, slam and crater, keeping the same total
per tier, with a further 18% to everything caught in the crater. Vanilla hurt immunity is reset per
beat, since three hits land inside one ten-tick window.

## Camera

The old shot resolved a shared midpoint between two locked bodies. That cannot work here — they end
up twenty blocks apart vertically, and the midpoint would sit in empty sky. Each participant's
camera rides their own body instead; because both are on the same vertical column, both shots still
contain the whole action. The cut is: low behind the fist → hard kick up on the uppercut → the one
wide plate for the column → high and tight over the drop → hitstop → blow out to the crater.

Fall damage and the 62-degree look-down are both cleared in `finishKingsGrip`, so an aborted run
cannot drop the attacker out of the sky staring at the floor.

## Conqueror's hold: rhythm reads as dancing

Two attempts failed for the same reason, and it is worth naming because it is not obvious. A smooth
low-frequency sway on body yaw and roll, mirrored left and right, is exactly what a person does to
music. Calling it a laugh or a convulsion does not change what the eye sees. **Pain has no beat.**

There is no sway in the clip at all now. The pose is fixed — hunched, curled in, both hands clamped

## Why it read as a hoop in the sky

The crater drew its shock wave as three billboards up to **forty-eight blocks across**. From
anywhere near the impact you are inside one, so it renders as a coloured ring hanging overhead

# Patch 19

## Billboards are why everything looked flat

Photon's default render mode is `Billboard`: every quad turns to face the camera. That is why a
sprite-heavy effect still reads as flat no matter how many sprites it has — all of them rotate
together, so there is **no parallax between them**. Adding particles cannot fix it; changing their
orientation can.

Photon also has `Horizontal` (flat on the ground), `Vertical` and `VerticalBillboard` (yaw-only,
like vanilla smoke). Those stay put as the camera moves, which is what gives an effect depth. The
Haki Grip now mixes all three:

| Layer | Mode | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shock wave, scorch, launch dust ring, slam pressure plate | `Horizontal` | Lying on the floor, and staying there — walking round the crater changes what you see |
| Dust dome, dust column, all fire and smoke trails, slam wake | `VerticalBillboard` | Each puff keeps its own facing; the difference between a cloud and a decal |
| Flash, sparks, embers | `Billboard` | Point lights genuinely should face you |
| Debris pillars, ground lightning | beams | Oriented quads in world space, unchanged |

## The crater was firing into a block

The launch column was built off the **attacker's feet**. On any slope that is not the floor under
the column, so the victim was driven either to a stop a block in the air or straight into terrain —
and a crater fired inside a block is completely invisible. The floor height under the column is now
sampled once at commit, by clipping downward, and the whole column stands on that.

## Actually in the hand

Three things had to line up, and two out of three still leaves it floating:

1. **The pose needs a knowable hand.** The clip holds the arm dead straight along the facing now,
   no yaw swing, almost no elbow bend — the one pose whose hand position is exactly derivable. The
   previous yaw swing alone accounted for about a third of a block sideways, which is precisely the
   offset in the screenshot.
2. **The offsets are the model's.** Pivot 5 texels out and 2 below the body top; limb 12 texels; so
   a straight arm puts the hand 0.75 ahead of it. All scaled by the real body height.
3. **An arrow's origin is not the middle of its shaft.** The renderer spans it roughly −0.68 to
   +0.22 along its facing — the origin sits near the head and the shaft trails behind. A shot
   arriving points at the player, so that trailing shaft hung in front of the hand. The origin is
   pushed forward by half the span so the fist closes on the middle.

# Patch 20

## Overriding a bone means owning it everywhere

Four attempts at posing the Conqueror hold failed, each for its own reason: the legs folded, then
`body` took the legs with it, then the torso curl put the spine somewhere no spine goes. The lesson
is not that the poses were bad. **Any bone a clip writes is a bone vanilla stops handling** — in
every state the player can be in: walking, sneaking, swimming, standing on a slab, mid-fall. Vanilla
already gets all of those right, and an authored value replaces that outright.

So the clip animates exactly one bone. The head goes back to look at the sky and twitches there,
on three incommensurate frequencies with occasional sharper spasms. Nothing else is touched.

The `torso` channel is present but flat zero, and that is not posing: the resource validator
requires a BendyLib `bend` key somewhere in every clip, and the head has no bend state.

## The crater, addressed to a place

It kept not appearing, and the reason was structural rather than a bad constant. Every visual in the
mod is addressed by **entity id**: the client has to resolve that entity before it can draw
anything, and the packet only reaches players tracking it. That is right for an aura on a body and
wrong for an explosion — and a body that has just been driven sixty blocks into the floor is exactly
the case where the lookup goes stale on the tick it matters.

`S2CWorldFx` addresses a **coordinate**, sent to everyone within 128 blocks of it via
`PacketDistributor.NEAR`, and drawn from coordinates that cannot go stale. `HakiImpactScreen.quakeAt`
does the same for the tremor.

## Advanced cracks the floor

At the Advanced tier the landing now leaves the same fracture the full-charge Conqueror release
does, at a smaller radius — one main break plus three offset chips so it is not one tidy stamp,
drawn `Horizontal` so it reads as damage to the floor rather than a decal in the air.

# Patch 21

The caught arrow is held at **one fixed angle**, always the same one: tip forward, down and a little
across the body.

Keeping the direction it flew in on was the wrong call and is dropped. That angle differs for every
shot — from above, from the side, on a steep arc — so the catch never looked the same twice, and
roughly half the time the shaft pointed at or away from the viewer and collapsed into a few pixels.

The orientation is set by giving the arrow a hair of velocity along the grip direction rather than by
writing `xRot`/`yRot`. `AbstractArrow#tick` recomputes its rotation from its motion every tick and
lerps toward the result, so a written angle gets dragged back out — but an arrow always renders along
the way it is travelling, so choosing the velocity chooses the picture, and it holds.
